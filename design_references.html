A

Cards & list rows

Live · hover me
Reference A: Homeroom student-list cards & KPI cards. Note the 12px border radius, the soft raised shadow, and the subtle hover state — a 3px lift into the overlay shadow over 220ms ease-in-out. Rows pack an avatar, monospace ID, grade badge, direction-colored attendance sparkline, and a status pill. Stat cards carry a colored top-accent line in the module hue.
Why it matters: the card is the atom of every EdForge dashboard. Get its radius, padding, shadow, and hover exactly right and 90% of the product inherits it.

B

Sidebar expand / collapse

Live · toggle it
Reference B: Sidebar animation on expand/collapse. Width animates over 220ms ease-in-out — lively but never jarring. Labels fade on a shorter 150ms opacity step so text never smears during the width change. The active item is a filled green pill; icons scale on hover.
Why it matters: the rail is on screen the entire session. A janky or over-eager collapse reads as cheap; the calibrated 220ms glide reads as infrastructure.
Screenshot slot — capture the expanded & collapsed states.
C

Icon animations on hover

Live · hover me
Reference C: Icon hover motion. 150ms scale (to 1.15) on smooth ease-out, the glyph adopts its module accent color, and a faint accent glow blooms behind it. This is the lightweight sibling of the signature icon-motion engine (--icon-dur .52s) used on nav.
Why it matters: the accent-per-module hover is EdForge's signature — it makes the product feel alive without a single distracting movement while the operator is reading.
Screenshot slot — GIF/capture of the nav icon bloom.
D

Button hierarchy

Live · hover me
Reference D: Button hierarchy. Primary is deep-green solid (#0F6E56); hero/brand CTAs use the teal→cyan gradient. Secondary is outlined. Danger is rust. Pill buttons carry navigation & filters. In forms, primary buttons go full-width. All at 8px radius with 150ms feedback; disabled drops to muted fill and loses elevation.
Why it matters: one clear primary per view keeps operators certain of the next action. Ambiguous button weight is how school software gets people lost.

E

Modal card treatment

Live · open it
Reference E: Modal treatment. Centered on screen, dimmed backdrop with a 12px blur, the dialog on the modal elevation at 18px radius. Minimal chrome — one title, generous padding, a single quiet close affordance top-right. Enters with fade-in + a 16px slide-up; no bounce.
Why it matters: modals interrupt. The blur focuses attention, the restraint respects it — the operator is one glance from knowing what's being asked and one click from resolving it.

F

Color palette in context

Reference
Reference F: The palette across light and dark regions. The Coolors brand ramp (teal → cyan → aqua → golden → caramel → rust) plus module accents. The split panel shows how the same semantic roles — Active / Warning / Overdue and the green primary — re-tint per theme: deep, AA-safe foregrounds on the warm light canvas; bright foregrounds on the navy dark surface.
Why it matters: EdForge is vibrant but never garish. Colors must stay legible and consistent whether they land on #FAF7F2 or #0F1117 — always via semantic tokens, never raw hex.

G

Form input states

Live · focus a field
Reference G: Input states. One consistent field at 8px radius. Focused = cyan border + a 3px soft ring (220ms). Error = rust border, rust ring, and helper text with an icon. Disabled = muted fill, disabled text, no ring. Labels sit above; help/error text sits below.
Why it matters: data entry is where operators live. Unmistakable, uniform state feedback prevents the silent bad-record entry that plagues school systems.
Screenshot slot — the Enroll Student form with a live error.
H

Navigation pill buttons

Live · click a pill
Reference H: Navigation & filter pills. Segmented control with a fully-rounded track; the active pill fills deep-green with a soft glow, its count badge inverts, and the swap transitions over 220ms ease-in-out. Filter chips are quiet outlines that tint green when on.
Why it matters: these pills are the primary way operators slice a roster of hundreds. The active state must be instantly unambiguous and the transition calm, never a flicker.
